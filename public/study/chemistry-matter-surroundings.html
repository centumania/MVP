🌍The Big Picture

One idea runs the whole chapter: matter is tiny moving particles.

Ice, water and steam are the SAME particles — H₂O — with different amounts of freedom. Give particles energy (heat) and they vibrate, then flow, then fly. Every fact in this chapter — melting, evaporation, diffusion, even plasma — is that one idea wearing different clothes.

Why exams love itKelvin conversions, latent-heat logic (steam vs water burns), evaporation-cooling examples (matka, sweat) and sublimation lists are fixed features of SSC/TNPSC science sections.

🔬Branch 1 · Particle Nature of Matter

Four properties of particles

WhatMatter is made of particles that are (1) extremely tiny, (2) have spaces between them, (3) are in constant motion, (4) attract each other.
HowProofs: KMnO₄ colours water even after huge dilution (tiny); sugar dissolves without raising water level (spaces); perfume spreads (motion); a chalk breaks but an iron nail doesn't (attraction strength differs).
Exam"Which observation proves interparticle spaces?" → dissolving without volume rise.
Key gradientAttraction: solid > liquid > gas. Spaces: solid < liquid < gas. These two orders answer a dozen MCQs.

🧱Branch 2 · The Three States

Same particles, different freedom

Solid: particles locked, only vibrate → fixed shape + volume, incompressible. Liquid: particles slide past each other → fixed volume, container's shape, flows. Gas: particles fly freely → no fixed shape/volume, highly compressible, exerts pressure.

Real lifeGas compressibility is why an entire kitchen-month of fuel fits in one LPG cylinder.
Odd casesSponge compresses (air escapes pores — still solid). Rubber stretches (still solid). Salt takes the jar's shape as a heap, but each crystal keeps its own shape — still solid.

🔥Branch 3 · Change of State & Latent Heat

🫥 The hidden heat

WhatLatent heat = heat absorbed or released during a change of state with NO temperature change.
WhyAt the melting/boiling plateau, all supplied energy goes into breaking attractions, not into speeding particles up.
HowFusion (ice→water): 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg. Vaporisation (water→steam): 22.6 × 10⁵ J/kg.
ExamTwo classics: steam burns worse than boiling water (extra latent heat released on condensing); ice cools better than ice-cold water (absorbs latent heat while melting).
Ice −10°Cheat→0°Cplateau: melts at 0°Cheat→100°Cplateau: boils at 100°C→steam

🌨️ Sublimation

Some solids skip the liquid stage entirely: camphor, naphthalene, iodine, ammonium chloride, dry ice (solid CO₂). Naphthalene balls shrink to nothing without leaving a puddle.

Real lifeDry ice makes stage fog and keeps ice-cream cold in transport — it sublimes straight to CO₂ gas below 1 atm.

💨Branch 4 · Evaporation

🥶 Nature's air conditioner

WhatA surface phenomenon: high-energy particles at the liquid's surface escape as vapour — at any temperature.
WhyEscapees carry away extra energy → average energy of remaining particles drops → the liquid cools.
HowSpeed it up: ↑ surface area, ↑ temperature, ↑ wind, ↓ humidity.
WhereSweat cooling the body, matka water, acetone-cold palm, tea in a saucer, clothes drying, desert coolers.
Exam"Why do clothes dry slowly on a rainy day?" → high humidity slows evaporation.
TrapEvaporation ≠ boiling. Evaporation: any temperature, surface only, slow, causes cooling. Boiling: fixed temperature, whole bulk bubbles, fast.

🌡️Branch 5 · Temperature & the Kelvin Scale

K = °C + 273

Kelvin is the SI unit of temperature (no degree symbol). Water melts at 273 K (0°C) and boils at 373 K (100°C) at 1 atm. Absolute zero = 0 K = −273°C — the coldest possible temperature, where particles hold minimum energy.

Instant conversions25°C = 298 K · 27°C = 300 K · 37°C (body) = 310 K · −196°C (liquid N₂) = 77 K.

🌫️Branch 6 · Diffusion

Particles mix themselves

Diffusion = intermixing of particles of two substances on their own — direct proof that particles never stop moving. Fastest in gases, faster when hot (sugar in hot vs cold water).

Life runs on itFish breathe O₂ diffused into water; leaves exchange CO₂/O₂; hot-food aroma announces dinner from the kitchen.

🌌Branch 7 · Plasma & Bose–Einstein Condensate

The 4th and 5th states

Plasma: heat or electricity rips electrons off gas atoms → glowing ionised gas. The sun, every star, lightning, neon signs and fluorescent tubes are plasma. It's the most common state of visible matter in the universe.

Bose–Einstein condensate (BEC): a super-dilute gas chilled to a whisker above absolute zero — particles lose individuality and behave as one giant particle. Predicted from Satyendra Nath Bose's work with Einstein (1924-25); created in the lab in 1995 (Nobel Prize 2001: Cornell, Ketterle, Wieman).

HookPlasma = hottest extreme; BEC = coldest extreme. The five states in energy order: BEC → solid → liquid → gas → plasma.

⭐Branch 8 · Key Numbers & Applications

The fact bank

Latent heat of fusion (ice) = 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg; vaporisation (water) = 22.6 × 10⁵ J/kg. Gases liquefy under compression + cooling — that's how LPG (Liquefied Petroleum Gas) and liquid-ammonia tankers work. Ice floats because water expands on freezing — the famous density exception that lets lakes freeze top-first and fish survive below.

⚠️Confusion Pairs & Exam Traps

Where examiners hide the wrong options.

1. Evaporation vs Boiling

Evaporation: any temperature, surface only, slow, cools. Boiling: only at boiling point, whole bulk, fast. "Evaporation occurs only at 100°C" = classic false statement.

2. Latent heat vs Specific heat

Latent heat changes state (temperature constant). Specific heat changes temperature (state constant). A heating curve alternates: slope = specific, plateau = latent.

3. 273 vs 373

Melting = 273 K, boiling = 373 K. Options love swapping them. Anchor: 2 comes before 3, melting comes before boiling.

4. Kelvin has no degree symbol

Write 298 K, not 298°K. Also Kelvin can't be negative — the lowest is 0 K. "−5 K" in an option is automatically wrong.

5. Dry ice never melts (at 1 atm)

Solid CO₂ sublimes straight to gas at normal pressure. Liquid CO₂ exists only under high pressure. "Dry ice melts into liquid" = trap.

6. Sponge/rubber/salt are still solids

Compressible sponge (air pockets), stretchable rubber, shape-taking salt heap — all remain solids. State is decided by particle arrangement, not by everyday behaviour.

7. Gases have weight and exert pressure

"Gases are weightless" = false. They have mass; their colliding particles exert pressure on container walls — that's how tyres stay firm.

8. Diffusion vs Osmosis

Diffusion: any particles intermixing, no membrane needed. Osmosis: water/solvent through a semi-permeable membrane. Options swap them in biology-flavoured questions.

9. Density order has an exception

Usually solid > liquid > gas — but ice < water. If the question says "always", the answer is false because of water.

10. Cooling BY evaporation vs cooling OF evaporation

The liquid that evaporates cools what it leaves behind (skin, matka water) — the escaping vapour itself is not "cold". Wording traps hinge on this.

11. Plasma ≠ blood plasma

Physics plasma = ionised gas (4th state). Blood plasma = liquid part of blood. Same word, unrelated meanings — general-awareness papers exploit it.

12. Melting point measures attraction, not size

A higher melting point means stronger interparticle forces — not bigger particles or higher density. "Iron melts at high temperature because its particles are heavy" = wrong reasoning.
