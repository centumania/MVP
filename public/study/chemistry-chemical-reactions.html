🌐The Big Picture

Every reaction is atoms re-partnering. Learn to spot the pattern — combine, split, swap, or transfer oxygen/electrons — and every question becomes classification.
Change happens→ Write & balance the equation→ Classify the pattern→ Predict products
Anchor ideaAtoms are never created or destroyed — they only re-arrange. That's why equations must balance, and why every reaction fits one of a few patterns: combine (A+B→AB), split (AB→A+B), swap (displacement), or transfer O/e⁻ (redox).

🔄1 · Chemical Change

What
A change producing new substances — unlike a physical change (same substance, new form).
Why the signs appear
New substances have new properties — a different colour, a gas that wasn't there, an insoluble solid, released or absorbed heat. The signs are the new substances announcing themselves.
How
Signs: gas evolution, colour change, precipitate, temperature change, smell. Exothermic = heat out (burning, respiration); endothermic = heat in (photosynthesis).
Where
Cooking, digestion, rusting, curdling milk, fireworks.
Exam
"Which is a chemical/physical change?" is guaranteed. Candle = both (melt physical + burn chemical). Respiration = exothermic; photosynthesis = endothermic.

⚖️2 · Equations & Balancing

What
A chemical equation writes a reaction in symbols: reactants → products, with state symbols (s), (l), (g), (aq).
Why balance
Law of conservation of mass (Lavoisier): atoms can't appear or vanish, so each element's count must match on both sides.
How
Hit-and-trial: balance metals first, then non-metals, then H, then O — adjusting coefficients only, never formulas. E.g. H₂ + O₂ → H₂O becomes 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.
Where
Every reaction question, stoichiometry, lab work.
Exam
"Balancing satisfies conservation of mass." "(aq) = dissolved in water." "You may change coefficients, never subscripts."

🤝3 · Combination Reactions

What
Two or more substances form one product: A + B → AB.
Why often exothermic
Forming new bonds releases energy — so combinations like burning and slaking of lime pour out heat.
How
CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + heat (quicklime slaking). 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (dazzling white). C + O₂ → CO₂.
Where
Whitewashing walls, burning fuels, striking a match.
Exam
"Quicklime + water → slaked lime, exothermic." "Wall shines: Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃." "Clean Mg ribbon with sandpaper first."

💥4 · Decomposition Reactions

What
One compound splits into simpler substances: AB → A + B — powered by heat, light or electricity.
Why energy is needed
Breaking bonds costs energy, so decomposition must be fed energy — the three feeders name the three types.
How
Thermal: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂; green FeSO₄ → brown Fe₂O₃ (sulphur smell); Pb(NO₃)₂ → PbO + brown NO₂ + O₂. Photolytic: 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂ (white→grey). Electrolytic: 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (H₂ double O₂, at cathode).
Where
Cement/lime kilns, B&W photography, hydrogen production.
Exam
The four classics above ARE the questions — colours, gases and smells included. H₂:O₂ = 2:1; hydrogen at cathode.

ThermalHeatCaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂

🔀5 · Displacement Reactions

What
Single: a more reactive element evicts a less reactive one (A + BC → AC + B). Double: two compounds swap ions (AB + CD → AD + CB).
Why the reactivity series rules
Only a metal higher in the series can push out a lower one — reactivity is the muscle behind every displacement.
How
Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu (blue→green, nail browns). BaCl₂ + Na₂SO₄ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NaCl (white precipitate). Neutralisation is also a double displacement.
Where
Thermite welding, metal refining, qualitative salt tests.
Exam
"Fe + CuSO₄ observations." "BaSO₄ white precipitate." "Precipitation = double displacement." "Can Cu displace Zn? Never."

🔥6 · Redox Reactions

What
Oxidation = gain of O / loss of H / loss of e⁻. Reduction = the exact opposite. They always happen together.
Why always together
Electrons (or oxygen atoms) don't vanish — what one substance loses, another must gain. One reaction, two halves.
How
CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O: CuO reduced (black→brown), H₂ oxidised. The oxidised substance is the reducing agent; the reduced one is the oxidising agent — agents are named for what they do to the OTHER.
Where
Metal extraction, batteries, combustion, corrosion, bleaching.
Exam
"Identify oxidised/reduced in CuO+H₂ or ZnO+C." "Oxidised = reducing agent" (the top trap)."

🏠7 · Everyday Effects: Corrosion & Rancidity

What
Two slow oxidations in daily life: corrosion attacks metals; rancidity attacks fats and oils in food.
Why they happen
Oxygen is relentless — given moisture and time it oxidises iron to rust; given warmth and light it oxidises cooking oil to smelly acids.
How to prevent
Corrosion: paint, oil, galvanise, alloy. Rancidity: antioxidants, airtight packs, refrigeration, and nitrogen-flushed chips packets.
Where
Rusting gates, stale chips, rancid butter, blackened silver.
Exam
"Chips packets → nitrogen." "Rancidity = oxidation of fats." "Corrosion = metals; rancidity = food."

🌈8 · Famous Reactions & Colour Changes

What
The colour-and-smell bank examiners draw from every year — learn each observation as a mini-story.
Why colours matter
In an exam you're given the observation and asked for the chemistry (or vice versa). Colours are the fingerprints of reactions.
How
FeSO₄: green → brown + sulphur smell. Cu: heated → black CuO; +H₂ → brown Cu. AgCl: white → grey in light. Pb(NO₃)₂: → brown NO₂ + yellow PbO. Lime water: milky with CO₂, clears with excess.
Where
Lab practicals, photography, whitewash, gas tests.
Exam

🧠Memory Engine

One hook per recall-worthy fact. Concept → hook → recall question.
Reaction patterns peg
"Combine, Split, Swap, Transfer" — combination, decomposition, displacement, redox. Every reaction is one of the four verbs.
Q: AB → A + B is which verb? → Split (decomposition).
OIL RIG classic
"Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain" — of electrons. For oxygen it flips: oxidation gains O.
Q: Loss of electrons is? → Oxidation.
Agent naming trap-buster
"The one oxidised is the reducing agent" — agents are named for what they do to the OTHER substance.
Q: In CuO + H₂, the reducing agent is? → H₂.
தமிழ் — வினை வகைகள் tamil hook
"சேர் (combination), பிரி (decomposition), இடம்மாற்று (displacement)." வெப்பம் வெளியே = வெப்ப-உமிழ் (exo); உள்ளே = வெப்ப-கொள் (endo).
Q: ஒளிச்சேர்க்கை exo-வா endo-வா? → Endothermic (வெப்பம் உள்வாங்கும்).
FeSO₄ colour story visual
"Green leaves burn brown, and it smells of burnt sulphur." Green FeSO₄ → brown Fe₂O₃ + SO₂/SO₃.
Q: Residue colour after heating FeSO₄? → Brown.
Lead nitrate fumes visual
"Brown smoke, yellow ash." Pb(NO₃)₂ → brown NO₂ fumes + yellow PbO residue.
Q: Brown fumes on heating lead nitrate are? → NO₂.
AgCl photo-trick story
"Silver salt tans in the sun" — white AgCl turns grey as light frees the silver. That's photography's grandfather.
Q: AgCl in sunlight becomes? → Grey (Ag + Cl₂).
Electrolysis ratio number peg
"H₂ is 2, O₂ is 1 — hydrogen at the cathode." Water splits 2:1 by volume.
Q: H₂:O₂ volume ratio in water electrolysis? → 2:1.
Quicklime chain chain
"Limestone → Quicklime → Slaked lime → back to Limestone": CaCO₃ –heat→ CaO –water→ Ca(OH)₂ –CO₂→ CaCO₃. The wall completes the circle.
Q: Whitewash shine is which compound? → CaCO₃.
Chips packet gas everyday
"Chips ride on a nitrogen pillow" — inert N₂ keeps oxygen off the oil, stopping rancidity.
Q: Gas inside chips packets? → Nitrogen.
Corrosion vs Rancidity peg
"Metal rots = corrosion; food fat rots = rancidity." Both are slow oxidation — different victims.
Q: Oxidation of oils in food is called? → Rancidity.
Balancing rule rule
"Touch the big numbers, never the small ones." Coefficients may change; subscripts never.
Q: Can you change H₂O to H₂O₂ while balancing? → Never.

⚠️Exam Traps & Confusion Pairs

Each has been set in real papers. Read the reality line and it dies.
Trap 1 · Oxidised = REDUCING agentThe substance that gets oxidised is the reducing agent (it reduces the other). Naming it the "oxidising agent" is the #1 error here.
Trap 2 · Photosynthesis is ENDOthermicPlants absorb light energy. Respiration and burning are exothermic; photosynthesis is not.
Trap 3 · Coefficients, never subscriptsBalancing changes the big numbers in front — turning H₂O into H₂O₂ creates a different compound.
Trap 4 · AgCl decomposes by LIGHT, not heatWhite→grey AgCl in sunlight is photolytic, not thermal decomposition.
Trap 5 · Hydrogen at the CATHODEIn water electrolysis H₂ collects at the cathode (−) and is double the O₂ volume. Both facts get swapped.
Trap 6 · Neutralisation is double displacementAcid + base → salt + water is a partner-swap, not a combination reaction.
Trap 7 · Quicklime vs slaked lime vs limestoneCaO quicklime, Ca(OH)₂ slaked lime, CaCO₃ limestone/marble. The trio is endlessly shuffled in options.
Trap 8 · FeSO₄ heating coloursGreen → brown (Fe₂O₃), with SO₂+SO₃ smell — not white, not black.
Trap 9 · Displacement obeys the seriesCu can never displace Zn or Fe. Only a HIGHER metal displaces a lower one.
Trap 10 · Rancidity is oxidation, not bacteriaRancid oil is a chemical oxidation problem — fixed by antioxidants/N₂, not by boiling.
Trap 11 · Milkiness clears with EXCESS CO₂Lime water turns milky with CO₂ but turns clear again when excess CO₂ forms soluble bicarbonate.
Trap 12 · A candle shows BOTH changesWax melting = physical; wax burning = chemical. "Only physical" and "only chemical" are both wrong.
Assertion–ReasonA: Silver chloride is kept in dark bottles. R: Light decomposes it into silver and chlorine. → Both true, R explains A.
Assertion–ReasonA: Quicklime water hisses and gets hot. R: Slaking of lime is highly exothermic. → Both true, R explains A.
