🌍The Big Picture

How scientists mapped something they could never see.

An atom is a tiny dense positive nucleus (protons + neutrons) surrounded by electrons in shells — and it is 99.9% empty space. This chapter is a detective story: Thomson found the electron, Rutherford found the nucleus, Chadwick found the neutron, and Bohr explained why the atom doesn't collapse.

The nuclear atom: dense centre, orbiting electrons, mostly empty space.
Why exams love itParticle discoverers, gold-foil conclusions, Z/A calculations, 2n² and octet, isotope-vs-isobar, and α/β/γ penetration are guaranteed SSC/TNPSC/RRB material.

🔴Branch 1 · Subatomic Particles

The three building blocks

WhatElectron (−1, Thomson 1897), proton (+1, Goldstein/Rutherford), neutron (0, Chadwick 1932).
WhyProtons give the nucleus its positive charge; neutrons add mass and hold the nucleus together; electrons balance the charge and do the chemistry.
HowProton ≈ neutron ≈ 1 u; the electron is ~1836× lighter, so nearly all mass sits in the nucleus.
Exam"Who discovered the neutron?" → Chadwick. "Which particle is neutral?" → neutron.

🔬Branch 2 · Early Models

🥇 The gold-foil experiment

Thomson imagined a "plum pudding" — electrons dotted through a positive sphere. Rutherford tested it by firing alpha particles at thin gold foil. The result stunned everyone:

Most pass straight through→atom mostly empty|a few bounce back→tiny dense + nucleus
Rutherford's flawA revolving electron should continuously radiate energy, spiral inward and make the atom collapse in an instant. Classical physics couldn't save it — enter Bohr.

🪐Branch 3 · Bohr Model

Fixed shells fix the collapse

WhatElectrons revolve only in certain stationary orbits (shells K, L, M, N) without radiating energy.
HowEnergy rises outward (K lowest). An electron absorbs energy to jump out and emits a photon to fall in — the origin of line spectra.
Exam"Shells are named…" → K, L, M, N. "Electron falling to a lower shell…" → emits energy.

🔢Branch 4 · Atomic & Mass Numbers

Z, A and the neutron count

Atomic number Z = number of protons — the element's fingerprint. In a neutral atom, electrons = protons = Z. Mass number A = protons + neutrons (the nucleons). So neutrons = A − Z.

Notation: mass number on top, atomic number below — ²³₁₁Na means sodium, 11 protons, 12 neutrons.

One equation, many questionsGive any two of (protons, neutrons, mass number) and you can always find the third.

🧅Branch 5 · Shells, Configuration & Valency

🧮 2n² and the octet

Each shell holds a maximum of 2n² electrons: K=2, L=8, M=18, N=32 — filling inner shells first. Atoms are stable with a full octet (8 in the outer shell; 2 for K = duplet), the noble-gas configuration.

Valence electrons (outermost) decide chemistry. Valency = outer electrons if ≤4, else 8 − outer electrons. Noble gases: 0.

👬Branch 6 · Isotopes & Isobars

Same-something, different-something

Isotopes: same Z, different A (different neutrons) — chemically identical, physically different. Hydrogen's protium/deuterium/tritium; carbon-12/13/14. Isobars: same A, different Z (different elements) — ⁴⁰Ar and ⁴⁰Ca. Isotones: same neutron count.

Why 35.5?Chlorine's fractional atomic mass is the weighted average of Cl-35 (75%) and Cl-37 (25%): (75×35 + 25×37)/100 = 35.5 u.

☢️Branch 7 · Radioactivity

🔴🔵🟢 Alpha, beta, gamma

Discovered by Becquerel (1896); the Curies found polonium and radium. Unstable nuclei emit three radiations:

🔴 Alpha (α)

Helium nuclei, +2, heaviest, stopped by PAPER, most ionising

🔵 Beta (β)

Fast electrons, −1, stopped by ALUMINIUM

🟢 Gamma (γ)

EM waves, no charge, need thick LEAD, most penetrating

📏 Order

Penetration: γ > β > α · Ionising: α > β > γ

⭐Branch 8 · Key Numbers & Uses

The applied fact bank

Cobalt-60 → cancer radiotherapy. Iodine-131 → thyroid diagnosis/treatment. Carbon-14 (half-life ~5730 yr) → dating fossils. Uranium-235 → fissile nuclear fuel (U-238 is the abundant non-fissile isotope). The Bohr–Bury filling rule: outer shell ≤ 8, next-to-outer ≤ 18, and electrons enter a new shell only after these limits are met.

🧠Memory Engine

Hooks, mnemonics and Tamil tricks — one per hard-to-hold fact.
Discoverers TRCMnemonic
Thomson-electron, Rutherford-nucleus, Chadwick-neutron. "தாம்சன் மின்னணு, ரூதர்போர்டு கரு, சாட்விக் நியூட்ரான்" — T-R-C in discovery order.
Asked as: who discovered the neutron? Chadwick.
1836Number hook
Proton is 1836× heavier than an electron. Think "18-36" like a countdown; the electron is the featherweight of the atom.
Asked as: proton:electron mass ratio? ~1836.
Gold-foil resultStory
Rutherford: "as if a shell fired at tissue paper bounced back." Most alphas pass (empty atom); a few rebound (tiny dense nucleus). "பெரும்பாலும் காலி, நடுவில் அடர் கரு."
Asked as: gold-foil proved the existence of? The nucleus.
KLMN shellsAlphabet hook
Shells start at K (not A) and go K-L-M-N. Why K? Historically the 11th letter left room for inner shells. Just remember "K comes first, closest to the கரு."
Asked as: name of the first shell? K (n=1).
2n² capacitiesNumber hook
2-8-18-32. Double the squares: 2×1², 2×2², 2×3², 2×4². "இரண்டு-எட்டு-பதினெட்டு-முப்பத்திரண்டு."
Asked as: max electrons in M shell? 18.
A = p + nFormula hook
Mass number counts only the heavy nucleus dwellers (protons + neutrons). Electrons are too light to matter. Neutrons = A − Z. "கனமானவை மட்டும் எண்ணு."
Asked as: neutrons in ²³Na? 23 − 11 = 12.
Z is identityConcept hook
Change protons → change element. Z is the atom's ID card; neutrons only change the "weight class" (isotope). "புரோட்டான் மாறினால் தனிமமே மாறும்."
Asked as: what defines an element? Atomic number Z.
Valency mirrorLogic hook
Outer ≤4 → valency = outer. Outer >4 → valency = 8 − outer. Na(1)→1, Cl(7)→1. "நான்கு வரை நேரடி, மேலே 8 கழி."
Asked as: valency of an atom with 6 outer electrons? 2.
Isotope vs IsobarWord hook
IsoTOPe — same Protons (top of periodic identity). IsoBAR — same mass (a 'bar' of equal weight). "டோப்-புரோட்டான், பார்-நிறை."
Asked as: same mass, different element = ? Isobars.
Cl = 35.5Number hook
75% of 35 + 25% of 37 = 35.5. The half comes from mixing two isotopes — no atom weighs 35.5, it's an average. "சராசரி, ஒரு அணுவும் அல்ல."
Asked as: why is Cl mass 35.5? Isotope average.
α β γ penetrationTamil hook
Penetration γ > β > α; stoppers paper → aluminium → lead. "ஆல்பா-காகிதம், பீட்டா-அலுமினியம், காமா-ஈயம்." Heavy α is easiest to block.
Asked as: which is stopped by paper? Alpha.
Isotope usesStory
Cobalt-60 fights Cancer; Iodine-131 for the thyroid (iodine loves thyroid); Carbon-14 for dating old Carbon-based life; U-235 for nuclear power.
Asked as: isotope for cancer therapy? Cobalt-60.
Rutherford's crashConcept hook
A spinning charge radiates and spirals in — Rutherford's atom should die in 10⁻⁸ s. Bohr said "no, orbits are fixed and silent." "சுழலும் மின்னணு விழும்; போர் நிலையான ஓடு."
Asked as: Bohr's fix to Rutherford? Fixed non-radiating orbits.

⚠️Confusion Pairs & Exam Traps

Where examiners hide the wrong options.

1. Who discovered what

Electron = Thomson. Nucleus = Rutherford. Neutron = Chadwick. Bohr gave the SHELL model, not a particle. Options swap these names constantly.

2. Mass number vs atomic mass

Mass number (A) is a whole number (protons + neutrons). Atomic mass can be fractional (35.5 for Cl) because it averages isotopes. Don't equate them.

3. Mass number excludes electrons

A = protons + neutrons ONLY. Electrons are ~1/1836 u — too light to count. "Mass number = protons + electrons" is a planted error.

4. Neutrons = A − Z (not A + Z)

Subtract, never add. For ²³Na: 23 − 11 = 12 neutrons. Adding gives an impossible 34.

5. Isotope vs Isobar

Isotopes: same Z (element), different A. Isobars: same A, different Z (different elements). The word decides: TOPe = protons, BAR = mass.

6. Isotopes are chemically identical

Same electron arrangement → same chemistry. They differ only physically (mass, density, nuclear stability). "Isotopes react differently" = false.

7. 2n² vs outer-shell cap

2n² gives the theoretical maximum (M = 18), but the OUTERMOST shell never exceeds 8 at a time (Bohr–Bury). Both rules are true simultaneously — options exploit the tension.

8. Alpha is least penetrating but most ionising

Heavy, +2 alpha barely penetrates (paper stops it) yet ionises the most. Gamma is the reverse — deeply penetrating, weakly ionising. Options flip these.

9. Alpha = helium nucleus, not helium atom

Alpha is He²⁺ (2 protons + 2 neutrons, NO electrons). Calling it a "helium atom" or "proton" is wrong.

10. Radioactivity discoverer

Becquerel discovered radioactivity (1896); the Curies discovered the elements polonium and radium. Don't credit the Curies with the phenomenon itself.

11. Absorb vs emit energy

Jump OUT (to higher shell) = absorb energy. Fall IN (to lower shell) = emit a photon. The direction and the verb must match.

12. Isotope-use mix-ups

Cobalt-60 = cancer (not power). Uranium-235 = fuel (not therapy). Carbon-14 = dating (not medicine). Iodine-131 = thyroid. Options rotate the pairings.
