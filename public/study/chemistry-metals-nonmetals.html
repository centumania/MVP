🌐The Big Picture

One split drives everything: metals lose electrons, non-metals gain them. Properties, reactivity, bonding, extraction and corrosion all follow.
Metals lose e⁻→ Reactivity series ranks them→ Bonding & extraction follow→ Corrosion & alloys fix problems
Anchor ideaA metal's whole personality comes from its loose outer electrons: they carry heat and current (conduction), let layers slide (malleability), reflect light (lustre), and leave easily (forming positive ions and reactions). Non-metals hold electrons tightly — so they're the opposite in every column.

✨1 · Physical Properties

What
Metals: lustrous, malleable, ductile, sonorous, good conductors, high melting points. Non-metals: dull, brittle, insulators, low melting points.
Why metals conduct & shine
Their free electrons ferry charge and heat, and re-emit light from the surface — one electron sea explains conduction, lustre and malleability all at once.
How
Malleable = sheets (gold leaf); ductile = wires (1 g gold → ~2 km wire); sonorous = ringing (bells).
Where
Copper wires, aluminium foil, iron structures, gold jewellery, metal bells.
Exam
"Most malleable/ductile = gold." "Best conductor = silver." "Bells are metal → sonority." Every exception below is a PYQ.
The famous exceptions — memorise as a setMercury: liquid metal. Bromine: liquid non-metal. Graphite: conducting non-metal. Diamond: hardest natural substance. Iodine: lustrous non-metal. Na/K: soft metals cut by knife. Tungsten: highest melting point.

⚗️2 · Chemical Reactions

What
Metals react with oxygen (basic oxides), water (hydroxide/oxide + H₂) and acids (salt + H₂). Non-metal oxides are acidic.
Why some react and some don't
It all depends on how easily the metal gives up electrons. Sodium surrenders instantly (violent with cold water); copper holds on (no reaction even with steam).
How
Water ladder: K/Na → cold water; Ca → warm; Mg → hot; Al/Zn/Fe → steam; Cu/Ag/Au → never. Acids: metals above H give H₂. Amphoteric oxides (Al₂O₃, ZnO) react with both acids and bases.
Where
Hydrogen 'pop' test, magnesium ribbon burning white, sodium fireworks in the lab demo.
Exam
"Fe reacts only with steam." "Cu gives no H₂ with dilute acid." "Amphoteric = Al₂O₃, ZnO." Displacement: Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu.
The iron nail experimentDip an iron nail in blue CuSO₄ solution: the nail turns brown (copper coats it) and the solution fades to green (FeSO₄). One experiment proves iron is more reactive than copper — a guaranteed exam picture.

📶3 · The Reactivity Series

What
Metals ranked by how readily they react: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > (H) > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au.
Why it predicts everything
The series is really an electron-giving ranking. Whoever gives electrons more easily wins every contest: displaces lower metals, releases H₂ from acids, corrodes first.
How
Above H → releases hydrogen from acids. Higher metal displaces lower from its salt. Top metals never occur free; bottom metals (Au, Ag) occur native.
Where
Predicting reactions, choosing extraction methods, galvanising logic, thermite welding.
Exam
"Most reactive metal in options" (usually K/Na). "Least reactive" (Au). "Can Cu displace Zn? No." H is the reference line.

🔗4 · Ionic Bonding

What
Metals lose electrons (→ cations), non-metals gain them (→ anions); the electrostatic attraction between them is the ionic bond.
Why the transfer happens
Both atoms want a full outer shell. Na (2,8,1) dumps one electron; Cl (2,8,7) grabs it — both reach stable octets and the resulting Na⁺Cl⁻ pair locks together.
How
Ionic compounds are hard crystals, have high melting points, dissolve in water and conduct only when molten or dissolved (never as solids).
Where
Table salt, washing soda, most salts and metal oxides.
Exam
"Metals form + ions." "Ionic compounds don't conduct as solids." "High melting points." NaCl electron-transfer diagram.

⛏️5 · Extraction of Metals

What
Getting metal from its ore. The method matches the metal's reactivity: electrolysis (top), carbon reduction (middle), heating alone (bottom).
Why three methods
The harder a metal grips its compound, the more force is needed to free it. Na/Al bond so strongly that only electricity can rip them out; Fe/Zn yield to hot carbon; Hg lets go with mere heat.
How
Roasting = sulphide ore + excess air; calcination = carbonate ore, limited air. Then reduce the oxide. Thermite: Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃ (rail welding).
Where
Bauxite→Al smelters, haematite→Fe blast furnaces, cinnabar→Hg, rail-track welding.
Exam
Ore matching (bauxite-Al, haematite-Fe, cinnabar-Hg, galena-Pb). "Na extracted by electrolysis." "Roasting vs calcination." Thermite reaction use.

🦀6 · Corrosion

What
Slow eating-away of metals by air/moisture. Iron rusts (Fe₂O₃·xH₂O), copper greens, silver blackens.
Why iron needs BOTH air and water
Rusting is an electrochemical process: oxygen is the oxidiser, but water is the medium that carries ions. Remove either (oil layer, dry air) and rust cannot form.
How
Prevention: paint, oil, grease, chrome plating, galvanising (zinc coat) and alloying (stainless steel). Zinc protects sacrificially — being more reactive, it corrodes first even if scratched.
Where
Bridges, car bodies, ship hulls, water pipes, the green Statue of Liberty.
Exam
"Rust needs air + moisture." "Rust = Fe₂O₃·xH₂O." "Copper → green basic carbonate; silver → black sulphide." "Galvanisation = zinc coat, sacrificial."

🥇7 · Alloys

What
An alloy is a homogeneous mixture of a metal with other metal(s) or carbon — engineered for better properties.
Why alloys are harder
Foreign atoms of a different size disturb the neat layers of the pure metal so they can't slide — the alloy becomes harder and stronger than its ingredients.
How
Steel = Fe+C; stainless steel = Fe+Cr+Ni; brass = Cu+Zn; bronze = Cu+Sn; solder = Pb+Sn; amalgam = metal+Hg. 22K gold = gold + Cu/Ag for hardness.
Where
Utensils, statues, coins, electrical solder, dental fillings, jewellery.
Exam
Alloy matching is a guaranteed question: brass/bronze/steel/solder/amalgam compositions.

⭐8 · Important Metals & Exceptions

What
The static-GK layer of this chapter: record-holders, abundances and the exception list examiners recycle every year.
Why exceptions are tested
Rules are easy; exceptions separate rankers. Every "which of the following is NOT…" question lives here.
How
Mercury liquid metal; bromine liquid non-metal; Al most abundant metal; O most abundant element; tungsten highest m.p.; Na/K knife-cuttable, kerosene-stored; graphite conducts; diamond hardest.
Where
Thermometers (Hg), bulb filaments (W), aircraft (Al), pencils (graphite).
Exam
Every fact in the How row is a repeated one-mark PYQ. Learn them as a single "exceptions card".

⚠️Exam Traps & Confusion Pairs

Each has been set in real papers. Read the reality line and it dies.
Trap 1 · Silver conducts best, not gold/copperThe best conductor is silver; copper is used in wires only because silver is costly. Gold's records are malleability/ductility.
Trap 2 · Mercury vs BromineMercury = only liquid metal; bromine = only liquid non-metal. Options love swapping them.
Trap 3 · Graphite conducts, diamond doesn'tBoth are carbon, but only graphite has free electrons. "Diamond conducts electricity" is false.
Trap 4 · Metal oxides basic, non-metal oxides acidicReversing this is a standard wrong option. The exceptions are the amphoteric pair: Al₂O₃ and ZnO.
Trap 5 · Copper gives NO hydrogenCu (and Ag, Au) are below H in the series — they release no H₂ from dilute acids or water.
Trap 6 · Displacement goes one wayFe displaces Cu from CuSO₄, but Cu can never displace Fe (or Zn). Lower can't push out higher.
Trap 7 · Iron reacts with steam onlyNot cold water, not hot water — steam. Na/K take cold water; Mg takes hot.
Trap 8 · Rust needs BOTH air and moistureOnly oxygen, or only water, is not enough. Rust = Fe₂O₃·xH₂O, not FeO.
Trap 9 · Brass-Zn, Bronze-SnBrass = Cu + Zn; bronze = Cu + Sn (tin). The single most swapped alloy pair.
Trap 10 · Roasting vs CalcinationRoasting = sulphide + excess air; calcination = carbonate + limited air. Match S-with-roaSting.
Trap 11 · Most abundant: element vs metalOxygen is the most abundant element; aluminium is the most abundant METAL. Questions bank on the mix-up.
Trap 12 · Ionic solids don't conductIonic compounds conduct only when molten or dissolved. "NaCl solid conducts electricity" is false.
Trap 13 · Aluminium's oxide protects itAl is reactive, but its oxide layer shields it — that's why Al doesn't corrode away like iron.
Trap 14 · Tin cans, not zinc cansFood cans are tin-plated because tin is LESS reactive than zinc — safer with food acids.
Assertion–ReasonA: Gold ornaments are made of 22K, not 24K gold. R: Pure gold is too soft; Cu/Ag harden it. → Both true, R explains A.
Assertion–ReasonA: A galvanised bucket resists rust even when scratched. R: Zinc, being more reactive, corrodes in place of iron. → Both true, R explains A.
