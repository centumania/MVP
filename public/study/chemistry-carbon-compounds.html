🌍The Big Picture

Why one element gets an entire chapter to itself.

Food, fuels, clothes, medicines, plastics, your own body — all built on carbon. One element makes millions of compounds, more than all other elements combined. This chapter answers: how does one small atom manage that? Two superpowers: tetravalency (4 bonds every time) and catenation (it bonds to itself endlessly).

Why exams love itFormulas (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ etc.), ethanol/ethanoic acid reactions, soap vs detergent, and diamond vs graphite appear in nearly every SSC/TNPSC science paper.

🔗Branch 1 · Bonding in Carbon

🤝 Covalent bonds — carbon shares

WhatCarbon forms bonds by sharing electron pairs — covalent bonds.
WhyWith 4 valence electrons, losing 4 (C⁴⁺) or gaining 4 (C⁴⁻) both cost enormous energy. Sharing is the only affordable option.
HowEach shared pair = one bond. In CH₄, carbon shares one pair with each of 4 hydrogens.
WhereAll organic compounds; also H₂, O₂, N₂, H₂O, NH₃.
Exam"Carbon compounds have low melting/boiling points and don't conduct electricity — why?" Answer: covalent (no ions, weak intermolecular forces).

4️⃣ Tetravalency & ⛓️ Catenation

Tetravalency: carbon always makes exactly 4 bonds. Catenation: carbon bonds to other carbons, building chains, branches and rings of any length.

Together they explain the millions of compounds. Silicon also catenates, but Si–Si bonds are weak (bigger atom, longer bond) — so silicon chains snap. Carbon's small size → short, strong bonds.

TrapCatenation ≠ tetravalency. Catenation = self-linking; tetravalency = 4 bonds. Examiners swap the definitions.

⛽Branch 2 · Hydrocarbons

➖ Saturated vs 🟰 Unsaturated

Hydrocarbons = carbon + hydrogen only. Saturated (alkanes): all single C–C bonds — "full" of hydrogen. Unsaturated: a C=C double bond (alkenes) or C≡C triple bond (alkynes) — could hold more H.

CH₄ methane→C₂H₆ ethane→C₂H₄ ethene (C=C)→C₂H₂ ethyne (C≡C)
Real lifeMethane = CNG, biogas, "marsh gas". Ethyne (acetylene) = welding torches. Ethene ripens fruit.
TrapIsomers (same formula, different structure) start only from butane C₄H₁₀ — propane and below have no isomers.

📶Branch 3 · Homologous Series

➕ The CH₂ ladder

WhatA family where each member differs from the next by CH₂ (14 u).
WhySame functional group → same chemistry; growing chain → gradually rising boiling point.
HowAlkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ · Alkenes CₙH₂ₙ · Alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂. Plug in n.
Exam"Which two belong to the same homologous series?" → check the general formula, not the size.
NamingPrefix = carbon count: meth(1), eth(2), prop(3), but(4), pent(5), hex(6). Suffix = bond/group: -ane, -ene, -yne, -ol, -oic acid.

⚗️Branch 5 · Reactions of Carbon Compounds

🔥 Combustion · ➕ Addition · 🔄 Substitution · ⚗️ Oxidation

Combustion: compound + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + heat. Saturated burn clean blue; unsaturated burn sooty yellow (more carbon %).

Addition (unsaturated only): H₂ adds across C=C with a nickel/palladium catalyst — this hydrogenation turns vegetable oil into vanaspati ghee.

Substitution (saturated only): in sunlight, Cl replaces H one at a time — CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl.

Oxidation: alkaline KMnO₄ or acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ (oxidising agents) convert ethanol → ethanoic acid.

TrapAddition needs a catalyst; substitution needs sunlight. Swapping these conditions is a favourite wrong option.

🍶Branch 6 · Ethanol & Ethanoic Acid

🍶 Ethanol (C₂H₅OH)

Liquid, mixes with water in all proportions. Made by fermentation of sugar (yeast). Used in drinks, tincture iodine, sanitisers, as fuel additive.

Reactions: + Na → sodium ethoxide + H₂ (pop) · + hot conc. H₂SO₄ (443 K) → ethene (dehydration) · + KMnO₄ → ethanoic acid.

Danger factDenatured alcohol = ethanol + methanol + dye (undrinkable). Methanol is oxidised in the liver to toxic methanal → blindness/death.

🥗 Ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH)

Vinegar = 5–8% solution in water. Pure acid freezes at 17°C → glacial acetic acid. A weak acid (weaker than HCl) but still turns litmus red, reacts with carbonates (CO₂ fizz) and with bases.

Esterification: CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH --(conc. H₂SO₄)→ ethyl ethanoate (sweet-smelling ester) + water. Esters → perfumes, flavours. The reverse with NaOH = saponification.

🧼Branch 7 · Soaps & Detergents

🫧 How washing actually works

WhatSoap = Na/K salt of long-chain fatty acids (fat + NaOH → soap + glycerol = saponification).
HowEach molecule has a hydrophobic tail (loves oil) and a hydrophilic head (loves water). Tails bury into grease, heads face water → spherical micelle lifts the dirt away.
WhereHard water (Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺) kills soap — insoluble scum, no lather. Detergents (sulphonate/ammonium salts) form soluble Ca/Mg salts → work anywhere, but many are non-biodegradable.
Exam"Why does soap fail in hard water but detergent doesn't?" — the single most-asked question from this branch.

⭐Branch 8 · Allotropes & Key Facts

💎 Diamond · ✏️ Graphite · ⚽ Fullerene

Same element, different structures = allotropes. Diamond: every C bonds 4 others in a rigid 3-D net → hardest natural substance, insulator. Graphite: hexagonal sheets with a free 4th electron → soft, slippery, conducts electricity. Fullerene C-60: football-shaped cage (buckminsterfullerene).

Fuel factsLPG = butane (+propane). CNG & biogas = methane. Welding = ethyne (oxy-acetylene).
Trap"Diamond conducts electricity" — FALSE. Only graphite conducts (and it conducts heat too). Diamond conducts heat exceptionally, not current.

Hooks, mnemonics and Tamil tricks — one per hard-to-hold fact.

Start at alkane +2 (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂), drop 2 H for each extra bond: alkene ±0 (CₙH₂ₙ), alkyne −2 (CₙH₂ₙ₋₂). Think "+2 → 0 → −2".

Asked as: butyne has which bond? Triple (C≡C).
Addition vs SubstitutionHook
Addition = Alkene/Alkyne (unsaturated) + cAtalyst. Substitution = Saturated + Sunlight. Match the first letters.
Asked as: CH₄ + Cl₂ needs? Sunlight (substitution).
Flame coloursLogic hook
More carbon % → more unburnt carbon → soot. Unsaturated = carbon-rich = sooty yellow. Saturated = clean blue. "சுத்தமானது நீலம், அழுக்கு மஞ்சள்."
Asked as: which burns sooty — ethane or ethyne? Ethyne.
Ethanol reaction trioStory
Ethanol's 3 exits: meets Na → gives H₂ (pop); meets hot H₂SO₄ → loses water, becomes ethene; meets KMnO₄ → gets oxidised into vinegar's acid. "Na-பாப், H₂SO₄-தண்ணீர் பறி, KMnO₄-புளிப்பு."
Asked as: product of ethanol + alkaline KMnO₄? Ethanoic acid.
Glacial acetic acidNumber hook
17°C — remember "வினிகர் 17-இல் ஐஸ் ஆகும்" (vinegar's acid freezes at 17). Glacial = glacier = ice-like crystals.
Asked as: pure ethanoic acid freezes at? 290 K / 17°C.
Ester = perfumeSense hook
Acid + alcohol = sweet smell. Ester → scent-er. Reverse with NaOH = saponification (soap!). The same bond forms perfume forwards and soap backwards.
Asked as: sweet-smelling substance from acid + alcohol? Ester.
Micelle pictureVisual
Imagine a சூரியகாந்தி பூ (sunflower): petals (hydrophilic heads) face the water-sun; the dark centre hides the grease. Tail = எண்ணெய் பிரியன், head = தண்ணீர் பிரியன்.
Asked as: which end of soap attaches to oil? Hydrophobic tail.
Soap vs hard waterTamil hook
"கடின நீரில் Ca/Mg சோப்பை சாக்கடை (scum) ஆக்கும்" — Ca/Mg + soap = scum, no நுரை (lather). Detergent-க்கு எந்த தண்ணீரும் சரி.
Asked as: why no lather in hard water? Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ scum.
Diamond vs GraphiteHook
Diamond = 4 bonds each = rigid = hard = no free electron = insulator. Graphite = 3 bonds each = 1 electron free = conductor, and loose sheets = slippery pencil lead. "4 கட்டு கடினம், 3 கட்டு மின்சாரம்."
Asked as: non-metal that conducts electricity? Graphite.
Fuel gasesRhyme
"LPG-la Butane, CNG-la Methane" — LB-CM. Biogas, marsh gas, natural gas — all methane family. Welding wants ethyne.
Asked as: main constituent of LPG? Butane.
CH₂ = 14Number hook
Homologous neighbours differ by CH₂ = 12+2 = 14 u — same as nitrogen's mass, "நைட்ரஜன் அளவு வித்தியாசம்".
Asked as: mass difference between propane and butane? 14 u.

⚠️Confusion Pairs & Exam Traps

Where examiners hide the wrong options.

1. Tetravalency vs Catenation

Tetravalency = 4 bonds. Catenation = self-linking chains. Both explain carbon's variety, but they are different properties — "carbon forms chains because of tetravalency" is a wrong statement.

2. Addition vs Substitution conditions

Addition: unsaturated + H₂ + Ni/Pd catalyst. Substitution: saturated + Cl₂ + sunlight. Options love swapping "catalyst" and "sunlight". Also: alkanes cannot do addition — nothing to open.

3. Ethanol vs Ethanoic acid tests

Sodium metal reacts with both (both have O–H) → NOT a distinguishing test. NaHCO₃/Na₂CO₃ fizz only with the acid → the distinguishing test. Litmus: only acid turns it red.

4. Ethene vs Ethyne vs Ethane

Ethane C₂H₆ (single), ethene C₂H₄ (double), ethyne C₂H₂ (triple). One letter changes the bond. Welding gas = ethyne; fruit-ripening = ethene.

5. Diamond conducts…heat, not electricity

Diamond: best natural conductor of heat, but electrical insulator. Graphite: conducts electricity. A statement mixing these is the classic trap.

6. Soap works in soft water only — detergent everywhere

But reverse trap: "detergents are always better" — no, many detergents are non-biodegradable (pollution). Soap is biodegradable.

7. Esterification vs Saponification

Esterification: acid + alcohol → ester (needs conc. H₂SO₄, gives perfume smell). Saponification: ester/fat + NaOH → soap + glycerol. They are near-opposites — forward makes scent, reverse makes soap.

8. Fermentation vs Distillation

Ethanol is made by fermentation (yeast on sugar); it is purified/concentrated by distillation. "Ethanol is prepared by distillation of sugar" = wrong.

9. Hydrogenation product ≠ healthier

Hydrogenated oil (vanaspati) is saturated fat — exam statements implying unsaturated oils become "more nutritious" on hydrogenation are false; saturated fats are considered less healthy.

10. Isomer starting point

Isomerism in alkanes starts at C₄ (butane) — methane, ethane, propane have no structural isomers. "Propane has two isomers" = false.

11. Methanol vs Ethanol danger

Ethanol = beverage alcohol (still harmful in excess). Methanol = poison — even small quantities cause blindness/death. Denatured alcohol is ethanol deliberately poisoned WITH methanol.

12. Glacial ≠ cold storage

"Glacial acetic acid" is not acid kept in ice — it is pure (water-free) ethanoic acid, named because it freezes at 17°C into ice-like crystals even in a cool room.
