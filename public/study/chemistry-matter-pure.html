🌍The Big Picture

"Pure milk" in an advertisement is chemically IMPURE.

In everyday talk, "pure" means unadulterated. In chemistry, pure means one kind of substance only. By that rule milk, honey, air, steel and sea water are all mixtures. This chapter sorts everything you see into elements, compounds and mixtures — then teaches the toolbox for pulling mixtures apart.

Why exams love itTyndall-effect examples, separation-technique matching, compound-vs-mixture (Fe+S experiment), alloy compositions and air percentages appear in every SSC/TNPSC cycle.

💎Branch 1 · Pure Substances vs Mixtures

The master classification

Matter→Pure: element / compoundorMixture: homogeneous / heterogeneous
WhatPure substance: one kind of particle (copper, water). Mixture: two or more substances physically together in any ratio.
HowHomogeneous = uniform, one phase (salt water, air, brass). Heterogeneous = visible parts (sand-water, oil-water, salad).
Exam"Which is a pure substance: milk / air / water / brass?" → water. Milk and brass fool many.

🥛Branch 2 · Solutions

🧂 Solute + solvent = solution

A solution is a homogeneous mixture: the solute dissolves in the solvent. Particles are < 1 nm — invisible, unfilterable, never settle, no light scattering. Solutions aren't only liquids: air (gas-in-gas) and alloys (solid-in-solid) count too.

ConcentrationMass % = solute mass ÷ solution mass × 100. (Dividing by solvent mass is the standard wrong option.)
Saturation & solubilitySaturated = holds no more solute at that temperature. Solids dissolve MORE in hot water; gases dissolve LESS — warm soda goes flat, warm lakes suffocate fish. Pressure ↑ keeps gas dissolved → soda bottles.

🌫️Branch 3 · Suspensions & Colloids

🔦 The three-mixture ladder

Suspension (>1000 nm): visible particles, settle on standing, filterable — muddy water, chalk-water. Colloid (1–1000 nm): stable, unfilterable, but scatters light — milk, fog, blood, starch solution. Solution (<1 nm): does none of these.

WhatTyndall effect = a light beam turning visible inside a colloid.
WhereSunbeam in a dusty room; headlights in fog; light shafts through forest canopy.
Exam"Which shows Tyndall: salt solution or starch solution?" → starch (colloid).

🧲Branch 4 · Separation Basics

Match the tool to the mixture

Evaporation: dissolved solid from liquid (salt from sea water). Filtration: insoluble solid (sand from water). Centrifugation: tiny suspended particles — cream from milk, blood tests. Separating funnel: immiscible liquids in layers — oil/water. Sublimation: camphor/NH₄Cl away from salt. Magnet: iron from sulphur.

Choosing logicAsk two questions: dissolved or not? liquid layers or not? The answers pick the technique for you.

⚗️Branch 5 · Advanced Separation

🌈 Chromatography → ⚗️ Distillation → 💠 Crystallisation

Chromatography ("colour-writing"): solutes climb paper at different speeds — ink dyes, plant pigments, drugs in blood. Simple distillation: miscible liquids, boiling points > 25 K apart (acetone + water). Fractional distillation: close boiling points, uses a bead-packed fractionating column — separating air into N₂/O₂/Ar, refining crude oil. Crystallisation: pure crystals from a hot saturated solution — beats evaporation because nothing chars and impurities stay behind.

Liquid air factCool + compress air to liquid, then warm slowly: N₂ (b.p. −196°C) escapes FIRST, then Ar, then O₂ (−183°C).

🔩Branch 6 · Elements & Compounds

🔥 The iron–sulphur classic

WhatElement: can't be broken down chemically (~118 known: metals, non-metals, metalloids). Compound: elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio with brand-new properties.
HowMix Fe + S: magnet still pulls iron; CS₂ still dissolves sulphur. Heat them → FeS forms: magnet fails, CS₂ fails, dilute acid now gives H₂S (rotten-egg gas) instead of H₂.
ExamWater = compound (H:O fixed at 1:8 by mass). Only liquid elements at room temp: mercury + bromine.

🔄Branch 7 · Physical vs Chemical Change

🕯️ One candle, two changes

Physical: no new substance, usually reversible — melting, dissolving, tearing, magnetising. Chemical: a new substance appears — burning, rusting, curdling, digestion. The candle does both at once: wax melts (physical) while wax vapour burns (chemical).

Spotting chemistryAny of these = chemical change: gas evolved, colour change, precipitate, heat/light released, can't be undone.

⭐Branch 8 · Alloys & Key Facts

The recycled fact set

Alloys are homogeneous MIXTURES (not compounds): brass = Cu+Zn, bronze = Cu+Sn, steel = Fe+C — ratios vary, properties blend. Air is a mixture: N₂ 78%, O₂ 21%, Ar 0.9%, CO₂ 0.04%. Water is a compound. 24-carat gold is pure; 22-carat ornament gold is alloyed for hardness. Tincture of iodine = iodine in alcohol; soda water = CO₂ in water.

⚠️Confusion Pairs & Exam Traps

Where examiners hide the wrong options.

1. Everyday "pure" vs chemical "pure"

"Pure milk", "pure honey", "pure ghee" — all chemically MIXTURES. Chemical purity = one substance. Water, salt, copper = pure; milk, air, brass = not.

2. Water vs Air classification

Water = compound (fixed 1:8 ratio, new properties). Air = mixture (variable ratio, gases keep properties, physically separable). Statements often swap them.

3. Alloys are mixtures, not compounds

Brass/bronze/steel have variable composition and blended (not brand-new) properties → homogeneous mixtures. But no, you can't filter the metals apart — physical separability doesn't mean EASY separability.

4. Mass % denominator

Concentration = solute ÷ SOLUTION × 100. With 20 g salt + 180 g water: 20/200 = 10%. The 11.1% option (20/180) is planted every time.

5. Colloids don't settle — suspensions do

Both are heterogeneous, but colloids are stable (milk never separates on standing). Settling + filterable = suspension only.

6. Tyndall needs colloid-size particles

True solutions (salt, sugar, CuSO₄) show NO Tyndall effect however concentrated. Starch solution — despite the name — is a colloid and DOES scatter.

7. Gas solubility runs backwards

Solids: hotter = more soluble. Gases: hotter = LESS soluble (flat warm soda, gasping fish), but MORE soluble under pressure. Two variables, opposite directions.

8. Separating funnel vs distillation

Funnel = immiscible (layers: oil/water). Distillation = miscible (no layers: acetone/water). "Separate alcohol from water with a funnel" = wrong — they mix completely (needs distillation).

9. Fractional vs simple distillation cutoff

Δb.p. > 25 K → simple. Δb.p. < 25 K → fractional (column). Air separation is ALWAYS fractional.

10. Crystallisation ≠ evaporation

Both recover solids, but crystallisation is the better technique: no decomposition (sugar would char), impurities left in the mother liquor.

11. Dissolving is physical, burning is chemical

Dissolving sugar/salt: recoverable → physical. But dissolving a metal in acid creates a new salt → chemical. Check whether a NEW substance exists.

12. Magnetising vs rusting

Magnetising an iron needle: same iron, reversible → physical. Rusting: new substance Fe₂O₃·xH₂O → chemical. Both happen to iron; options pair them deliberately.
