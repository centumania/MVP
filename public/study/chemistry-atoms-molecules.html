🌐The Big Picture

From two laws to counting atoms by weighing: laws → atoms → molecules → formulas → masses → the mole.
Two laws of combination→ Dalton's atoms explain them→ Formulas & masses→ The mole counts them
Anchor ideaAtoms are too small to count — but a fixed count of them (6.022×10²³, one mole) always weighs the atomic mass in grams. That single trick lets chemists count invisible particles with a weighing balance. Everything in this chapter builds toward it.

📜1 · Laws of Chemical Combination

What
Conservation of mass (Lavoisier): total mass is unchanged in a reaction. Constant proportions (Proust): a compound's elements are always in a fixed mass ratio.
Why both hold
If matter is made of indestructible atoms combining in fixed small numbers, mass must be conserved and ratios must be constant — Dalton's atoms explain both laws at once.
How
Water is always H:O = 1:8 by mass — from a river, the rain or a lab. Burn 3 g C in 8 g O₂ and you get exactly 11 g CO₂.
Where
Balancing equations, purity checks, stoichiometry.
Exam
"Conservation → Lavoisier; constant proportions → Proust." "Water = 1:8 by mass." Law-author matching is guaranteed.

🔴2 · Atoms & Symbols

What
The atom is the smallest particle of an element. Dalton gave the theory (1808); Berzelius gave letter symbols.
Why Latin symbols
Many elements were named centuries ago in Latin — their symbols froze from those names: Natrium, Kalium, Ferrum, Aurum, Ag (argentum), Pb (plumbum), Cuprum, Sn (stannum), Hg (hydrargyrum), W (wolfram).
How
1 u = 1/12 the mass of a C-12 atom. Key masses: H1, C12, N14, O16, Na23, Mg24, Al27, S32, Cl35.5, K39, Ca40, Fe56. Atom size ~10⁻¹⁰ m.
Where
Every formula and mass calculation that follows.
Exam
Latin-symbol matching is a repeated one-marker. "1 u defined on carbon-12." "Cl = 35.5."

🔗3 · Molecules & Ions

What
A molecule is the smallest freely-existing particle of a substance. An ion is a charged particle: cation (+) or anion (−).
Why atoms team up
Most lone atoms are unstable — they bond into molecules (H₂, O₂) or transfer electrons into ions to reach full outer shells. Only the noble gases are content alone (monoatomic).
How
Atomicity: mono (He, Ar), di (H₂, O₂, N₂, Cl₂), tri (O₃), poly (P₄, S₈). Polyatomic ions act as one unit: NH₄⁺, OH⁻, NO₃⁻, SO₄²⁻, CO₃²⁻, PO₄³⁻.
Where
Gas chemistry, salt formulas, electrolysis.
Exam
"Atomicity of O₃ = 3, P₄ = 4, S₈ = 8." "Cation positive, anion negative." Polyatomic-ion charges are formula gold.

✍️4 · Writing Chemical Formulae

What
Valency = combining capacity. The criss-cross rule swaps two valencies into subscripts to build any formula.
Why criss-cross works
The compound must be electrically neutral — swapping the valencies automatically balances total + and − charge.
How
Al(3) + O(2) → Al₂O₃. Mg(2) + Cl(1) → MgCl₂. With polyatomic ions, use brackets: Ca(OH)₂, (NH₄)₂SO₄, Ca₃(PO₄)₂.
Where
Every equation ever written; naming salts.
Exam
"Write the formula of X" — criss-cross does it in five seconds. Bracket errors are the marked trap.

⚖️5 · Molecular & Formula Mass

What
Molecular mass = sum of all atomic masses in the molecule (in u). For ionic compounds we say formula mass.
Why 'formula' for ionic
NaCl has no discrete molecule — it's an endless crystal lattice, so we compute the mass of one formula unit instead.
How
H₂O = 2+16 = 18 u; CO₂ = 44; NH₃ = 17; H₂SO₄ = 98; NaCl = 58.5; CaCO₃ = 100.
Where
Direct one-markers, and the first step of every mole numerical.
Exam
The five masses above (18, 44, 17, 98, 58.5) are asked verbatim — memorise them as numbers.

🐣6 · The Mole Concept

What
One mole = 6.022×10²³ particles (Avogadro's number) = the substance's atomic/molecular mass in grams (molar mass).
Why exactly that number
Nₐ is the number of C-12 atoms in exactly 12 g of carbon-12 — chosen deliberately so that masses in u become grams. That's the whole magic.
How
1 mol H₂O = 18 g = 6.022×10²³ molecules. Like "a dozen" = 12, "a mole" = 6.022×10²³ — a counting word for particles.
Where
All quantitative chemistry — dosages, industrial yields, gas volumes.
Exam
"Avogadro's number = 6.022×10²³." "Molar mass of water = 18 g." "1 mole = Nₐ particles" — three guaranteed facts.

🧮7 · Mole Calculations

What
Two formulas solve everything: n = mass ÷ molar mass, and particles = n × Nₐ.
Why the triangle
Mass, moles and particles form a triangle: mass ↔ moles via M, moles ↔ particles via Nₐ. Any question is one or two hops on the triangle.
How
36 g water → 36/18 = 2 mol → 2 × 6.022×10²³ = 1.2×10²⁴ molecules. Reverse: divide by Nₐ, multiply by M. One particle's mass = M ÷ Nₐ.
Where
Every numerical in this chapter and beyond.
Exam
"Moles in 88 g CO₂ → 2." "Atoms in 12 g C → Nₐ." "Mass of 1 H₂O molecule ≈ 3×10⁻²³ g."

⚠️Exam Traps & Confusion Pairs

Each has been set in real papers. Read the reality line and it dies.
Trap 1 · 1:8 by MASS, 2:1 by atomsWater's H:O ratio is 1:8 by mass but 2:1 by atoms/volume. Options bank on the mix-up.
Trap 2 · Lavoisier vs ProustConservation of mass = Lavoisier; constant proportions = Proust. Swapping the authors is standard.
Trap 3 · u is defined on C-12, not O-16The modern atomic-mass standard is carbon-12. Oxygen-16 is the OLD standard — a dated distractor.
Trap 4 · Cl is 35.5, not 35Chlorine's average atomic mass is 35.5 u (isotope mixture). NaCl = 58.5, not 58.
Trap 5 · Cation is POSITIVECation = + (metal loses electrons); anion = −. "Cation is negative" is a set wrong option.
Trap 6 · O₂ vs O₃ atomicityO₂ is diatomic; O₃ (ozone) is TRIatomic. And P₄ = 4, S₈ = 8.
Trap 7 · Brackets on polyatomic ionsCa(OH)₂ — the bracket multiplies the whole ion. Writing CaOH₂ or CaO₂H₂ is the marked error.
Trap 8 · Molar mass in GRAMSMolecular mass is in u; molar mass is the same number in grams per mole. 18 u vs 18 g/mol.
Trap 9 · Moles ≠ mass1 mole of O₂ (32 g) is heavier than 1 mole of C (12 g) — equal moles never means equal mass.
Trap 10 · Equal masses ≠ equal atoms1 g of hydrogen has 16× more atoms than 1 g of oxygen. Lighter element → more atoms per gram.
Trap 11 · 'Formula mass' for ionic compoundsNaCl has no molecule — use formula mass/unit. Saying "molecule of NaCl" is technically wrong.
Trap 12 · Variable valenciesCu shows 1 & 2, Fe shows 2 & 3 (ferrous/ferric), N shows 3 & 5. "Fixed valency" statements about them are false.
Assertion–ReasonA: 18 g of water and 44 g of CO₂ contain equal numbers of molecules. R: Each amount is exactly one mole. → Both true, R explains A.
Assertion–ReasonA: The symbol of potassium is K. R: It derives from the Latin name kalium. → Both true, R explains A.
