🌍The Big Picture

Your house is a chemistry lab — exams test whether you know what each bottle really is.

The medicine cabinet, kitchen shelf, bathroom rack and farm shed are all stocked with chemicals wearing everyday names. Competitive exams love the translation game: common name ↔ chemical identity ↔ why it works. This chapter is that dictionary, organised for recall.

Why exams love it"Milk of magnesia is…", "LPG smell is due to…", "Iodised salt prevents…" — these one-line GK questions repeat across SSC, TNPSC, RRB and Police exams every year.

💊Branch 1 · Drugs & Medicines

The four everyday drug classes

WhatAnalgesics relieve pain (paracetamol, aspirin, ibuprofen). Antibiotics fight bacteria (penicillin — Fleming, 1928). Antacids neutralise stomach acid (Mg(OH)₂, NaHCO₃). Antiseptics protect wounds (Dettol, tincture of iodine).
WhyAntacids work by simple neutralisation — a weak base vs stomach HCl. Antibiotics attack bacterial machinery, which is why they do nothing against viral colds.
ExamAspirin doubles as a blood thinner — given during heart attacks. Antiseptic (skin) vs disinfectant (floors — phenol, bleach) is a fixed question.

🍬Branch 2 · Food Chemistry

🧁 The kitchen shelf decoded

Preservatives: salt, sugar, oil, vinegar traditionally; sodium benzoate and potassium metabisulphite in packaged food. Artificial sweeteners for diabetics: saccharin, aspartame, sucralose.

Baking soda vs baking powder: soda is plain NaHCO₃ — used alone it leaves bitter Na₂CO₃. Baking powder = NaHCO₃ + tartaric acid: on wetting/heating it releases CO₂ (the fluff) and the acid neutralises the bitterness.

Fruit chemistryEthylene (ethene) gas ripens fruit — one ripe banana ripens the bunch. Cut apples brown by enzyme oxidation; lemon juice (acid) slows the enzyme.

🧂Branch 3 · Household Chemicals

Common name → chemical identity

Washing soda Na₂CO₃·10H₂O — laundry, softens hard water. Bleaching powder CaOCl₂ — bleaches cloth/paper, disinfects drinking water. Plaster of Paris CaSO₄·½H₂O — made by heating gypsum to 373 K; sets with water into hard gypsum (casts, idols, ceilings). Vinegar = 5-8% acetic acid. Common salt is the industrial mother of NaOH, Cl₂, washing soda and baking soda.

TrapPoP must be stored dry — moisture sets it into useless solid gypsum inside the bag.

🧼Branch 4 · Cleansing Agents

🫧 Surface chemistry that scrubs

Soap micelles: hydrophobic tails bury into grease, hydrophilic heads face water — rinse and the dirt sails away. Detergents beat soap in hard/acidic water (their Ca/Mg salts stay soluble) but many resist biodegradation. Toothpaste is mildly basic — it neutralises the bacterial acid that dissolves enamel below pH 5.5; fluoride hardens enamel. Shampoo = mild detergent tuned for hair.

💄Branch 5 · Personal Care

The chemistry you wear

Hand sanitiser: 60-70% alcohol denatures microbial proteins — pure alcohol works WORSE (evaporates before penetrating). Perfumes are largely esters — the sweet-smelling acid+alcohol compounds. Talcum powder = powdered talc, the softest mineral (Mohs 1). Sunscreen: ZnO/TiO₂ reflect UV; SPF rates UV-B protection.

🔥Branch 6 · Fuels & Fire Safety

🧯 The fire triangle

WhatFire needs fuel + heat + oxygen. Every extinguisher removes one corner: water removes heat, CO₂/blankets remove oxygen.
WhyLPG (butane) is odourless — ethyl mercaptan is added as a leak alarm. Yellow sooty flames mean incomplete combustion → soot + carbon monoxide.
WhereCO from heaters/geysers in closed rooms is the silent killer: odourless, binds haemoglobin ~200× more strongly than O₂.
ExamElectrical fire → CO₂ (never water: shock). Oil-pan fire → smother with lid (never water: steam explosion throws burning oil).

🌾Branch 7 · Farm Chemicals

🌱 NPK and its price

Nitrogen → leafy growth; Phosphorus → roots; K (potassium) → flowers, fruit, disease resistance. Urea CO(NH₂)₂ carries 46% N — the world's favourite fertiliser, and historically the first organic compound made from inorganic matter (Wöhler, 1828).

Pesticides: insecticides (insects), herbicides (weeds), fungicides (fungi). DDT is banned — it persists and biomagnifies up food chains. Excess fertiliser runoff triggers eutrophication: algal bloom → decay → oxygen crash → dead fish.

⭐Branch 8 · Safety & Adulteration

🕵️ Chemistry protecting health

Iodised salt (KI/KIO₃) prevents goitre. ORS = salt + sugar + water — glucose co-transports sodium through the gut wall, pulling water in; it saves diarrhoea patients. Adulteration tests: iodine turns starch-in-milk blue-black; a lactometer exposes water in milk; metanil yellow in dal/turmeric is a toxic dye.

Never do thisBleach + toilet acid = toxic chlorine gas. Fuel burning in closed rooms = CO. Store household chemicals away from food bottles.

CO the silent killerNumber hook

Fire triangleVisual
Fuel + heat + O₂ — remove ANY side and fire dies. Water steals heat; CO₂/lid steals oxygen. Electrical → CO₂; oil pan → lid; "தண்ணீர் எண்ணெய்க்கு எதிரி."

"அயோடின் இல்லாத உப்பு = கழுத்து வீக்கம்" — iodised salt (KI/KIO₃) feeds the thyroid, preventing goitre. Cheapest public-health win.
Asked as: iodised salt prevents which disease? Goitre.

⚠️Confusion Pairs & Exam Traps

Where examiners hide the wrong options.

1. Antiseptic vs Disinfectant

Same job (kill microbes), different battlegrounds: antiseptic on living skin (Dettol, iodine), disinfectant on non-living surfaces (phenol, bleach). Concentration often decides — dilute phenol was once an antiseptic; strong phenol is a disinfectant.

2. Antibiotic vs Antiseptic

Antibiotics work inside the body against bacteria (swallowed/injected). Antiseptics work on surfaces of the body. And neither touches viruses.

3. Baking soda vs Washing soda

Baking = NaHCO₃ (hydrogencarbonate). Washing = Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (carbonate). Washing soda is stronger and inedible — the options always offer both formulas.

4. Plaster of Paris vs Gypsum

PoP = CaSO₄·½H₂O (active, powder). Gypsum = CaSO₄·2H₂O (set, hard). Heating gypsum makes PoP; water reverses it. Half vs two waters — that's the whole question.

5. LPG vs CNG

LPG = butane (liquefied, cylinders, heavier than air — sinks and pools). CNG = methane (compressed, vehicles, lighter than air — escapes upward). The smell in LPG is added, not natural.

6. CO vs CO₂

CO₂ suffocates only at high levels and puts OUT fires. CO is toxic at tiny levels — binds haemoglobin. Yellow flames and closed rooms produce CO; blue flames produce CO₂.

7. Water is not a universal extinguisher

Water on electrical fire = shock. Water on oil fire = steam explosion. Water is only for ordinary solids (paper, wood, cloth).

8. N-P-K job swap

N→leaves, P→roots, K→flowers/disease. Options rotate them ("potassium promotes root growth" — false, that's phosphorus).

9. Urea facts pair

46% nitrogen AND Wöhler's 1828 synthesis — two separate questions from one molecule. Don't attach Wöhler to any other compound.

10. Preservative vs Sweetener vs Colour

Sodium benzoate = preservative. Saccharin/aspartame = sweetener. Metanil yellow = illegal colour. Options shuffle these three roles.

11. Sanitiser strength paradox

60-70% alcohol beats 100% — some water is needed to penetrate and denature microbe proteins. "Stronger is better" is the planted wrong intuition.

12. Iodine's many tests

Iodine detects starch (blue-black) — in milk adulteration, potato, leaf-photosynthesis tests. But iodine in SALT is nutrition (goitre prevention), and tincture of iodine is an antiseptic. Same element, three exam roles.
